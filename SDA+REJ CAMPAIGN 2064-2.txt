Rally 1 — AYK
A large crowd gathers in a central public square. The atmosphere is energetic, louder than the earlier Kalahooska events, with orange, pink, and white banners hanging from buildings overlooking the square
The logos of the Social Democratic Alliance and Rejuvenation Party appear on opposite sides of the stage. Yuan Kew walks onto the stage to loud applause. She waves briefly before stepping to the lectern.
"Thank you. Thank you all very much. You know, I was told before coming out here that I should be optimistic tonight. But optimism is not the same thing as pretending everything is fine."
The crowd cheers.
"For ten years, the Right has governed Caprica. Ten years. And what have they done with it?"
The crowd begins shouting answers.
"They have taken powers away from the regions. They have cut budgets for essential public services. They have cut taxes for the wealthiest people in this country and then turned around and asked working people to carry the burden."
Applause spreads through the square.
"And every election, they come back and tell you the same thing. Just wait. Wait for the tax cuts to create prosperity. Wait for private investment to reach your community. Wait for the market to fix the problems that the government has spent ten years refusing to fix. My friends, managed decline is not a plan."
Loud applause.
"Caprica is a rich country. Look around you. We have some of the most talented people in the world. We have universities. We have industries. We have scientists, engineers, workers, doctors, teachers, entrepreneurs. So why does it feel like so many ordinary people are being asked to settle?”
The crowd quiets.
"Why are regional governments being asked to do more with less? Why do working families pay more while the wealthy are told they need another tax cut? Why do young people graduate into an economy that tells them opportunity exists — but often somewhere else?"
A murmur moves through the audience.
"The problem is not that Caprica has run out of potential." Yuan Kew leans slightly toward the lectern. "The problem is that we have run out of patience with people who keep telling us that potential is enough."
Applause rises.
"Now, let me be very clear. We are not here to promise that every problem disappears the day after an election." A brief pause. "We're here to tell you that the direction of this country can change. We can restore power to the regions, because the people living in a community should not have to beg distant politicians for permission to solve their own problems."
The crowd cheers.
"We can rebuild public services, because healthcare, transportation, education and housing are not luxuries that should depend on the wealth of your neighborhood. We can make the economy work for the people who actually make it work."
Applause.
"And we can finally ask something that the Right seems to have forgotten. Who is the economy for?"
The crowd begins cheering.
"Is it for the billionaire who can buy another apartment in a city they don't live in?"
"No!"
"Is it for the corporation that receives another tax concession while local services are cut?"
"No!"
"Or is it for the people who get up in the morning, go to work, raise their children, build our communities and make this country run?"
"Yes!"
“We believe in a Caprica where growth means something — where success is not measured only by GDP, but by whether people can afford to live in the country they are helping to build. Where a region can invest in its own future. Where a child does not have to be born into wealth to have access to opportunity. Where a small business is not expected to compete against a monopoly with lawyers, lobbyists and half the government in its pocket."
Some laughter and cheering.
"And where a government does not treat the people as an inconvenience."
The crowd cheers loudly. Yuan Kew takes a moment before continuing.
"This is not about moving backwards. It is about understanding that Caprica cannot enter the future with a government that still believes the solution to every problem is to cut, privatize and wait."
A sustained round of applause follows. She takes a drink of water, sets it down, and continues, her tone steadier now.
"Let me tell you what a serious government looks like, because for ten years, we haven't had one." Scattered cheers of agreement. "A serious government does not treat every newcomer to this country as a threat, and it does not treat every crisis at our border as an excuse to disappear into slogans. Caprica needs people. We need the doctors, the engineers, the tradespeople who choose to come here and build a life. And when we say we want a sensible immigration system, we mean exactly that — sensible. A clear path for people who have work waiting, who have something to contribute, and the decency to treat them like human beings while they get here instead of like paperwork."
Applause.
"And to anyone fleeing tyranny, fleeing a crisis they did not create, looking for nothing more than safety — Caprica will not slam the door in your face and call it strength. We will welcome you as what you are. People. Not a headline. Not a talking point for the Right to frighten people with on television."
Loud, sustained applause. She pauses to let it settle.
"Now. Let's talk about where Caprica stands in the world." A shift in the crowd's attention. "For ten years, this country's foreign policy has swung between pretending we're untouchable and panicking every time a stronger power raises its voice." Some laughter. "We reject both."

Applause.
"Caprica is a sovereign country. Not a lapdog for Alania. Not a lapdog for the OURS. A sovereign country that builds its relationships on goodwill, not on who shouts the loudest."
"But sovereignty does not mean standing alone while authoritarianism spreads next door." Her voice hardens slightly. "We see what the Norwyn government and its allies are doing to our neighbors, to the Union, to San Doria, to Kuliga. And we will not pretend it is none of our concern. A government under our leadership will stand with our allies against that kind of expansion, because appeasing it has never once made it smaller. It only waits for its next opportunity."
"And within the Columbian Union, we will keep saying what we have always said: solidarity does not mean uniformity. We do not believe Montiablo should force one economic model on every state at home, and we do not believe Maraiselle…  or anyone else… should force one economic model onto every country in the Union. Real solidarity defends each other's sovereignty. It doesn't erase it."
"Now, some of you are probably wondering why a rally about regions and working people is talking about satellites and cyber defence. Because national security is not just soldiers at a border anymore, and pretending otherwise is exactly the kind of thinking that has left Caprica a decade behind. The battles of this century are being fought in orbit and on a network, and Caprica has been comprehensively unprepared for both."
Some applause.
"So we will build what should have already existed. A new Office of Reconnaissance, to make sure Caprica is never blind in space when everyone else can see. A new Office for Electronic Engagements, to defend this country from threats that don't arrive by land, sea or air, but through a screen. And we will fund the education behind it — training a generation of young Capricans in the high-technology trades this century actually demands, instead of leaving them to learn it somewhere else and take that talent with them. That is what serious looks like. Not another slogan about strength. The actual work of building it."
Applause. She lets it settle, then softens slightly.
"And while we're building strength abroad, we are not going to forget the ground we stand on. For ten years, this country has treated the climate crisis as something to negotiate around instead of something to act on."
Applause.
"No new fossil fuel projects. Not one. Not while we still have the chance to choose differently."
"We will invest in the renewables that actually work, and we will push our researchers, our engineers, our universities to make the next generation of clean energy — hydrogen, biofuels, whatever comes next — cheaper and stronger than anything the fossil fuel industry has left to offer. We will work with our cities on cleaner air, on public transport people can actually rely on, so that choosing the bus over the car isn't a sacrifice — it's just the easier choice. And we'll do it in partnership with the regions who know their own streets better than any ministry in Montiablo ever will."
Some cheers.
"And yes — we will make it easier to do the small things too. Bring your bottles back. Bring your old electronics back. Because building a country that lasts isn't just about one big law. It's about a thousand small habits, made easy, done together."
Applause, warmer this time. She steps slightly away from the lectern, then returns.
"So let's bring this together since none of this is separate. It is one government, one direction, one answer to a single question. Who is Caprica for?"
The crowd begins cheering again.
"Is it for a government that cuts services and calls it discipline?"
The crowd answers ‘No!’.
"Is it for a government that ignores a warming planet and calls it caution?"
The crowd replies with ‘No!’ again.
"Is it for a government that treats the world as something to fear instead of something to stand in?"
The crowd responds with ‘No!’ once more.
"Or is it for the people who get up in the morning, go to work, raise their children, welcome their neighbors, build our communities, and make this country run?"
The crowd erupts. She nods, letting the cheering continue for a moment. 
"That's what we believe. We believe in a Caprica where growth means something, and where success is not measured only by GDP, but by whether people can afford to live in the country they are helping to build. Where a region can invest in its own future. Where a child does not have to be born into wealth to have access to opportunity. Where a small business is not expected to compete against a monopoly with lawyers, lobbyists, and half the government in its pocket."
Some laughter and cheering.
"And where a government does not treat the people, or the world beyond our borders… as an inconvenience."
The crowd cheers loudly. Yuan Kew takes a moment before continuing.
"It’s time we understand that Caprica cannot enter the future with a government that still believes the solution to every problem is to cut, privatize, and wait."
A sustained round of applause follows.
"The future will belong to the countries that invest in their people. The countries that educate their children. The countries that build modern infrastructure. The countries that develop their regions instead of abandoning them. The countries that defend their sovereignty without hiding from the world. The countries that understand economic strength and social strength are not enemies."
She steps slightly away from the lectern.
"For ten years, Caprica has been told to accept less. Less power. Less investment. Less government. Less ambition. Less of a place in the world."
Lee's voice rises.
"Well, we are here to offer something different. We are here to say Caprica deserves better."
The crowd begins rising.
"Not because it will be easy. Because we are capable of better." She raises a hand toward the audience. This country belongs to the people who built it. And if we stand together… that is, social democrats, socialists, workers, students, professionals, small business owners, newcomers who chose this country, every region of this country… then we can take its future back."
The crowd erupts into sustained applause.
"Caprica has been managed for ten years. Now it's time to build."
Lee waves as she steps away from the lectern. The crowd continues cheering as she shakes hands along the front of the stage.
"Thank you."


















Rally 2 — Zhaohua Li
The logos of the Social Democratic Alliance and Rejuvenation Party appear side by side on the stage. The atmosphere is warm rather than confrontational. Dr. Zhaohua Li walks onto the stage to sustained applause and waves to the audience before reaching the lectern.
"Thank you. Thank you all for being here."
She waits for the applause to settle.
"I have spent much of my life working in medicine, and one of the things medicine teaches you very quickly is that numbers can tell you what is happening, but they cannot always tell you what it feels like. You can see an economic statistic and think everything is improving, but that statistic cannot tell you about the person putting off a doctor's appointment because they're worried about the cost. It cannot tell you about the family waiting for better public transportation, or the student wondering whether their education will lead to a future they can actually afford."
A murmur of agreement moves through the crowd.
"That is why I believe governments must never forget the people behind the statistics."
Applause.
"For too long, politics in Caprica has become a conversation about systems without people. Budgets without families. Tax cuts without asking who actually pays for them in the end. Efficiency without ever asking whether a service is working for the person who depends on it."
A few people cheer.
"Now, I believe in efficiency. I'm a doctor. Give me an inefficient system and I will complain about it, believe me."
The crowd laughs and applauds.
"But fixing efficiency is not the same thing as cutting. Government should be efficient so that it can do more for people, not so it has an excuse to do less. A hospital that runs efficiently can care for more patients. A transport system that runs efficiently can connect more communities. A school system that runs efficiently can give more children a better education. That is the kind of government we believe in— not a government that asks, 'how much can we take away,' but one that asks, 'how much can we accomplish, together.'"
Loud applause.
"The Right has had ten years. Ten years in which we have watched essential services cut, regions stripped of authority, and ordinary people asked to quietly accept the consequences. Enough."
The crowd cheers.
"Caprica needs a government that understands public services are not charity. They are what we build together, because there are some things we should never leave to chance— your ability to see a doctor, your child's education, your ability to get to work in the morning, your community's ability to respond when disaster strikes."
Applause continues.
"That is why we will build a National Health Service, free at the point of use, for everyone, funded fairly, so that whether you can see a doctor depends on what's wrong with you and nothing else. Not your postcode. Not your bank balance. I have watched too many patients delay care they needed because they were doing the arithmetic in their head first. That should never be a decision a sick person has to make in this country."
Sustained applause.
"It is also why, here in Costa Blanca, we have not stopped building since the day this project began. Public housing that is still going up. Healthcare access that is still expanding. And I intend to keep building it, not because it makes a good headline, but because a family waiting for a home does not care what year the program started. They care whether it reaches them."
Cheers and applause.
"A government that cares also has to be honest about the cost of living, because you cannot tell a family their government cares about them while their weekly shop gets more expensive and their landlord raises the rent again. We will fight for a fairer tax system, one where the wealthiest finally pay their share instead of working families carrying it for them. We will bring VAT down on the essentials people cannot avoid buying. And we will make sure small businesses— the ones who actually hire your neighbors— are not left to compete against monopolies with more lawyers than employees."
Applause grows.
"A government that cares treats the climate crisis as a health crisis too, because it is one. The air a child breathes on their walk to school is a health outcome. The heat a construction worker endures in the middle of summer is a health outcome. So we will invest in cleaner public transportation, in renewable energy, in the kind of urban planning that gets cars off the road not by punishing people, but by finally giving them somewhere better to be. This isn't about asking ordinary people to bear the cost of a cleaner Caprica. It's about making sure they aren't the ones who have been bearing the cost of a dirtier one."
Warm applause.
"And a government that cares does not stop caring at the border. To the people who come to this country to work, to study, to build a life and contribute to it— you are not a statistic to be managed, you are welcome here. To those fleeing violence and tyranny they did not choose, Caprica will not turn its back and call it strength. I have treated patients in this country who crossed an ocean to get here. I have never once needed to ask them where they were born before treating them, and my government will not ask you to prove your worth before it treats you with basic decency either."
Sustained applause.
"None of this means Caprica stops looking after itself. A country that cares for its people also keeps them safe, and we will not apologize for that. But safety, real safety, is not just soldiers and slogans. It's a hospital bed that's there when you need it. It's a home that isn't one bad month away from being lost. It's a government that shows up before the crisis, not just after it."
Li steps back from the lectern for a moment before returning.
"Caprica does not need to become a different country. It needs to remember what kind of country it wants to be. A country where liberty means something. Where security means something. Where empathy is not weakness. And where no government ever forgets who it works for."
The applause becomes sustained, and Li smiles.
"I became involved in public life because I believe people deserve better. Not perfect." She pauses. "But better. And better is possible."
She looks across the audience.
"With your support, we can build it together."
The crowd erupts into applause as Li waves and steps away from the lectern.







Rally 3 – AYK
The rally takes place in an industrial city near a major transport hub. The stage is positioned against the backdrop of rail lines and factories. Large screens display images of Caprica's regions, its armed forces, research facilities and infrastructure. Behind the speaker are the words:
A STRONGER. SOVEREIGN. CAPRICA.
The crowd is large and politically mixed. Veterans stand beside students, workers and families. SDA and Rejuvenation banners are displayed throughout the venue. Alexandria Yuan Kew approaches the lectern as applause rises.
"Caprica is a small country. But being small does not mean being weak. And being weak is exactly what ten years of this government has made us.”
“For too long, powerful countries have looked at a country like ours and asked exactly one question. Which side are you on? Whose lapdog are you going to be? That is the wrong question, and any government that spends its energy answering it instead of asking what's actually good for Caprica has already surrendered before a single shot is fired. We will maintain our partnerships with the OURS and with Alania, and we will do it on our terms, not theirs. Caprica's foreign policy will be based on what serves Caprica. Not on which superpower flatters us the most this decade.”
“And to the Norwyn government, and to every authoritarian regime watching how far they can push before someone pushes back — hear this clearly. We will stand with the democracies you are trying to strangle. We will defend our allies, San Doria and Kuliga included, not with statements of concern, but with real cooperation and real capability. And we will make absolutely sure that the cost of your ambition outweighs whatever you think you stand to gain from it.”
“But strength is not just what happens on a map overseas. Strength begins here. With an economy that works. With people who are educated instead of left behind. With infrastructure that isn't held together with good intentions and duct tape. And with a government that finally understands the wars of the next decade will not look anything like the wars of the last one.”
“They will be fought in cyberspace. They will be fought through information, through disinformation, through networks most people never think about until the day they fail. They will be fought in orbit. And for ten years, this country has had a government that could not even secure its own intelligence apparatus, let alone prepare for any of that. They gutted the very institutions meant to keep this country safe and called it liberty. We call it what it was. Negligence, dressed up as principle.”
“An SDA–Rejuvenation government will modernize Caprica's national defense, and we will not apologize for it. We will invest in cyber capabilities that can actually withstand an attack instead of reacting to one after the damage is done. We will expand our satellite and reconnaissance capacity so that Caprica is never blind while everyone else can see. And we will make sure the next generation of Capricans has the skills to defend this country in a world that has already moved past the one this government has been governing for.”
“And we will do something this government has never once managed to do. We will prepare for the future before it becomes an emergency. That applies to security. It applies to technology. And it applies to the climate, whether the Right wants to admit that or not. No new fossil fuel projects during our term. Not one. We are done mortgaging this country's future to industries that have already taken everything they're going to give us. Instead, we invest in what will actually power the next fifty years. Renewables. Hydrogen. Biofuels. Scientific research that doesn't leave this country the moment it produces something valuable. Public transportation built for people, not for whoever can afford to avoid it.”
“Now, I know what they'll say. That this means another tax, another restriction, another lecture. No. We believe in making the cleaner choice the easier choice— transportation that actually works, cities built around people instead of traffic, technology that cuts waste instead of creating more of it, and industries capable of building the future right here, instead of watching it get built somewhere else while we argue about whether it's real.”
“This is what sovereignty actually means. It does not mean shouting the loudest into a camera. It does not mean isolating ourselves and calling it independence. It means having the strength to make our own decisions and the spine to live with them. Having the technology to defend ourselves instead of hoping someone else will. The economy to support ourselves instead of begging for it. And the confidence to cooperate with the world without kneeling to any part of it.”
“For ten years, Caprica has been governed by people with no ambition beyond staying in power. Now we need a government prepared to think ten years ahead of them, because I promise you — Norwyn is already thinking that far ahead of us, and every year we waste is a year we hand them for free. The challenges of tomorrow will not wait for an election cycle to be convenient. So neither will we.”
“We will defend this country. We will modernize this country. We will protect every inch of it, on the ground, in the network, and in orbit. And together, we will build a sovereign Caprica capable of meeting whatever the future brings — not because we hope for the best, but because for the first time in a decade, we will actually be ready for it.”
“Not a country living in fear of the future. A country that scares it right back."
The crowd roars as Yuan Kew steps away from the lectern, the applause continuing well after she has left the stage.
